## 3. District music

22.05 kHz mono, Vorbis q0.3. Every stem of a district has exactly the same number of samples,
`round(bars × 4 × 60 / bpm × 22050)`; for 104 and 110 BPM that is not an integer, so the loop is rounded to
the nearest sample (< 23 µs) and the beat grid is stretched by that amount so bar lines stay on the loop.
Events that ring past the loop end are wrapped to the start and all effects are circular, so each stem is
exactly periodic. Balance: stems are first set to relative loudness targets (`music.LOUDNESS`), then the
whole district is scaled by **one common factor** so the full mix of all stems (including tension and party)
peaks at -1 dBFS after decoding, with no stem above -0.3 dBFS.

Seam check (on decoded files): *jump/p99* = |x[end] − x[0]| divided by the 99th percentile of |x[n+1] − x[n]|
(≤ 1: the seam step is smaller than the signal's normal motion). *Click/beats* = 2nd-difference energy in a
3 ms window across the seam divided by the largest value found on the other 31 beat lines (drums hit
downbeats on purpose; a seam click would make this ≫ 1). *Codec edge* = Vorbis coding error in the first/last
10 ms relative to the 99th percentile of that error over the file (encoder edge artifacts would make it ≫ 1).
*In-scale %* = share of tonal spectral-peak energy (80 Hz–2 kHz) on the district's major scale; drum and
noise stems naturally score lower, and the jazz dominants deliberately use altered tones.
